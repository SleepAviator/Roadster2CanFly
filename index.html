BEHIND THE FLIGHT

A recreation, with its assumptions in view.

The two scenes follow the visible sequence in Tesla’s October 15, 2026 demonstration. Dimensions and engineering values here are adjustable assumptions; the clips are edited and do not provide calibrated trajectories.

What is simulated

Rapier integrates a 3D rigid chassis at 240 Hz. Gravity, quadratic drag, pressure-limited nozzle forces act during flight. Four spring-damper wheel contacts include friction and progressive bump stops. The driven approach and ramp adhesion are prescribed; they do not model Tesla’s suction system.

What the thrusters represent

Four nozzles each apply force at their mounting point: thrust equals mass flow × exhaust speed, opposite to the exhaust direction. Direction trims, valve limits, and firing windows affect those forces. The successful presets assume unrestricted automatic steering; choose “Fixed to car” to fire along a body-mounted direction.

A finite ideal-air tank stays at 293 K. A regulator caps inlet pressure at 100 bar absolute; flow and ideal expansion limit output as tank pressure drops. Air consumption reduces vehicle mass. Tank volume, initial pressure, rated flow, and speed limit are editable. These are assumed values, not a fitted Tesla cold-gas system. No CFD, real-gas cooling, plumbing losses, pressure-vessel structure, or nozzle geometry is solved.

What “successful” means

Avoid chassis collisions, touch down below 4 m/s vertical speed and 22° tilt, then stabilize on at least three wheels below 0.8 m/s forward speed. These are sandbox criteria, not vehicle safety limits. Peak wheel support is the modeled total wheel force divided by vehicle weight; it is not occupant acceleration.
